01 / AUTONOMOUS SYSTEMS SAE AERO

A ROBOT THAT HAS TO FIGURE OUT WHERE IT IS.

I work on the autonomous systems side of Texas A&M’s SAE Aero team. Our payload gets dropped from a plane, lands inside an 8 ft × 8 ft zone, and has to navigate on its own before reconnecting with the aircraft.

My work has touched the PCB, sensors, motor control, embedded C++, camera research, and navigation. The part I like most is that every decision affects something else. A sensor choice changes the software. A power decision changes the board. An algorithm is useless if the hardware cannot give it the data it needs.

02 / EMBEDDED DESIGN TIME-BLINDNESS WATCH

MAKING TIME VISIBLE.

For a design project, my group built a watch concept for people who struggle with time blindness. We used a GEMMA microcontroller and a NeoPixel ring so time could be represented visually instead of through another alarm or notification.

I owned the software side. I pushed for the watch to be customizable, because one fixed countdown pattern was not going to work for everyone. The idea was for a user to describe what they wanted, then translate that into specific LED colors, timing, and patterns the microcontroller could actually execute.

03 / RESEARCH EEG + RASPBERRY PI

TURNING BRAIN SIGNALS INTO SOMETHING I COULD ACTUALLY USE.

Over the summer, I worked on an EEG research project using a Muse S headband and Raspberry Pi. The goal was to stream and process EEG data and build a pipeline that could eventually support emotion or cognitive-state analysis.

I worked through Bluetooth connection issues, XDF recordings, filtering, ICA cleanup, annotations, and feature extraction. A lot of the project was learning how messy real sensor data can be, and how much work has to happen before a machine-learning model ever sees it.

HOW I WORK

Build.
Break.
Understand why.

01

Start with the system.

I try to understand what the hardware can actually sense, supply, and control before getting attached to an algorithm.

02

Ask early.

If I’m missing context, I would rather ask a team lead why an old decision was made than spend two days solving the wrong problem.

03

Test the physical thing.

Compiling is not the finish line. I care about what the board, sensor, motor, or dataset does when it is actually used.
